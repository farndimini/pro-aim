What Is Tracking Aim?

Tracking aim is the ability to keep your crosshair on a moving target continuously. Unlike flicking, which is a single explosive movement to acquire a target, tracking is a sustained, smooth motion that follows the target as it moves. It is the primary aiming skill required for games with high time-to-kill like Apex Legends, Overwatch 2, and Fortnite, but it is also important in tactical shooters for following strafing enemies during spray exchanges.

Tracking relies on different muscle groups than flicking. Flicking uses fast-twitch muscles for explosive movement. Tracking uses slow-twitch muscles for sustained, precise control. Most players are naturally better at one than the other, and identifying which one you are weak at is the first step to balanced aim improvement.

The 4 Types of Tracking

There are four distinct types of tracking that appear in different gaming scenarios. Smooth tracking involves following a target that moves in a predictable pattern, like a bot in a straight line. Reactive tracking requires you to follow a target that changes direction unpredictably, like a human opponent strafing. Predictive tracking means anticipating where the target will be and moving your crosshair ahead of time. Close-range tracking demands fast, wide movements to keep up with targets in your face.

5 Best Tracking Drills

  • Drill 1: Circle Tracking — Move your mouse in a perfect circle while keeping your crosshair on a fixed point. Do this for 2 minutes clockwise, 2 minutes counter-clockwise. This builds smooth circular motion control.
  • Drill 2: Strafe Tracking — In Pro Aim Classic mode, focus on following each target as it appears rather than flicking to it. Track the target for 0.5 seconds before clicking. This trains reactive tracking.
  • Drill 3: Precision Tracking — Use Precision mode but do not click. Simply follow each target with your crosshair for its entire lifetime. This isolates pure tracking from clicking.
  • Drill 4: Wide Swing Tracking — In Rapid Fire mode, practice tracking targets that appear at the edges of your screen. This trains the wide mouse movements needed for close-range tracking in Apex and Overwatch.
  • Drill 5: Anti-Mirror Tracking — Predict where the target will go next and place your crosshair there before the target arrives. This trains predictive tracking for projectile weapons.

How to Measure Tracking Improvement

Tracking improvement is harder to measure than flicking because accuracy percentages do not tell the full story. Instead, use the "center time" metric. In tracking scenarios, the percentage of time your crosshair spends within 10% of the target center is your center time. A center time above 70% indicates excellent tracking. Below 50% means you need more practice. Pro Aim does not currently measure center time, but you can estimate it by your accuracy in Rapid Fire mode. A Rapid Fire accuracy above 75% suggests good tracking fundamentals.

FAQ

Is tracking or flicking more important?

It depends on your game. For Apex, Overwatch, and Fortnite, tracking is more important. For Valorant and CS2, flicking and first-shot accuracy matter more. For balanced improvement, train both equally.

Why is my tracking shaky?

Shaky tracking is usually caused by tension in your grip or arm. Relax your hand. Use a lighter grip. If the shakiness persists, lower your sensitivity slightly.

Can I improve tracking without an aim trainer?

Yes. In any game, load a practice mode with moving bots and focus exclusively on following them with your crosshair without shooting. Do this for 5 minutes per session.
