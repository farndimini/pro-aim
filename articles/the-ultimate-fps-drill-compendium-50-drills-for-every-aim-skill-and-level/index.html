How to Use This Compendium

This compendium contains 50 aim drills organized into 7 categories. You do not need to do all 50 every day. Instead, use it as a menu. Pick 4-5 drills from different categories each day to create a balanced routine. Rotate drills every 2-3 weeks to keep your training fresh and avoid plateau. Each drill includes the time needed, difficulty level, and which games it transfers to. Bookmark this page and refer to it whenever you feel stuck or bored with your routine.

FAQ

Q: How many drills should I do per session?
A: 4-5 drills per session is optimal. More than 6 leads to diminishing returns from fatigue.

Q: How often should I change my drills?
A: Every 2-3 weeks. Your brain adapts to repeated stimuli. Changing drills forces new adaptation.

Q: Should I do the same drills every day?
A: No. Variation prevents plateau. Rotate through the categories so you train all skills evenly.

Q: What if a drill feels too easy?
A: Increase the difficulty by adding a time constraint, reducing target size, or adding movement.

Q: What if a drill feels too hard?
A: Slow down and focus on accuracy. Speed comes later. If a drill is too frustrating, drop to an easier variation and build up.

Q: Can I create my own drills?
A: Yes. The best drills target your specific weaknesses. If you notice you miss targets at a certain angle or distance, create a custom drill that replicates that scenario.
